###  Description

The [Center for Quantum and Topological Systems
(CQTS)](https://nyuad.nyu.edu/en/research/faculty-labs-and-projects/cqts.html)
at New York University Abu Dhabi (NYUAD) is seeking to recruit a Research
Assistant in the area of Quantum Computing, both at the hardware and the
software levels.

The center is an interdisciplinary research hub that involves the Computer
Science, Computer Engineering, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Physics programs.
Accordingly, research can be in either of two directions:

**1\. Quantum computing software**

With software being currently more theoretical, it is in a sense advancing
faster than hardware is, there is a need and prominence for quantum
simulators. It would hence be interesting and desirable to come up

with an approach to quantum computing languages that simulate quantum
processes not on a pertinent quantum computer but on a classical one.

At the same time, it would be important for such an approach to be fundamental
and aware of the quantum material underlying the expected hardware, leading to
languages that are implementable and realistic.

A promising such approach is through topological quantum computing, which is
robust against noise, errors, and perturbations, leading to reliable and
scalable hardware, and hence practically useful and implementable software.

The project will have several independent aspects to it, leading to the
possibility of working individually or as part of a team. The topics include
implementing and simulating (topological) quantum gates, verification and
certification of the simulations, building a reliable software library, and
then combining ingredients from such a library to construct simulations of the
desirable quantum programs.

**2\. Quantum computing hardware**

There has been a lot exciting research on building quantum computers using
various platforms using different physics and chemistry of the underlying
quantum materials. Many of the existing prototypes rely on extreme laboratory
conditions, such as large size which makes them immobile, and extreme
temperatures close to absolute zero, which make them physically inaccessible
for students.

However, one of the earliest approaches, which relies on spin resonance,
although operating on a small number of qubits, allows for such prototypes to
be of desktop size and operating under standard/normal conditions, rendering
them usefully accessible.

Another approach that is currently being developed is using topological
materials to utilize anyons for topological quantum computing.

The goal of the project is to explore hardware/materials for quantum computing
from various approaches, mainly spin resonance and topological quantum matter.

Simulations can be done on either GPU machines or on the CQTS dedicated
educational quantum computer, and there is an option of remotely accessing
various advanced quantum computing platforms.
